3. Information that stays on your device — and optional iCloud Sync

Everything the App works with is held locally, on your device, under your control, unless you turn on iCloud Sync as described below. If you delete the App, information held only inside the App is deleted with it, subject to how your device and any backup you maintain handle deleted app data, and to any copy kept in iCloud by iCloud Sync.

Optional iCloud Sync. iCloud Sync is off until you turn it on, on the iCloud Sync page when you first set up the App, by choosing to walk with a save held by another of your devices, or in the App’s Options › Permissions. It is not a condition of using the App, and each of your devices makes its own choice; an Apple Watch follows the choice made on its iPhone. If you turn it on, the App keeps the following in your own private iCloud database, so that your devices signed in to the same Apple Account can use it: your stroll under way and the strolls already brought home, including the Pokémon on them and the steps counted for them; your lifetime walking record, including step counts; the trainer cards of trainers you have met with Walker Connect; your Pokémon’s personality profiles; a copy of each save file you have chosen, the name of the device that holds it, and changes waiting to be written into it; and the App’s settings. ROM files are never synced.

End-to-end encryption. Before any of this data leaves your device, the App encrypts it with a key that is kept in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple protects with end-to-end encryption. Only your own devices signed in to your Apple Account can read it: neither Apple nor the Developer can. Your Apple Watch receives the key from its iPhone over their direct, encrypted connection, never through iCloud. The encrypted data is stored by Apple in your own iCloud account. It is not stored in any database the Developer can read, and the Developer cannot view, retrieve, decrypt, or delete it. Apple’s handling of it is governed by Apple’s terms and Privacy Policy for iCloud (see Section 11).

Keep iCloud Keychain on. Because only your devices hold the key, iCloud Sync depends on iCloud Keychain. A device that has not yet received the key from iCloud Keychain cannot read the App’s data in iCloud, and the App waits for it instead of replacing that data. If iCloud Keychain is turned off, if your Keychain is reset, or if the key is otherwise lost, your devices may permanently lose access to the App’s data in iCloud, and neither the Developer nor Apple can recover it. Each device keeps its own copy, and you may choose Start Afresh in iCloud in the App’s Options › Permissions to replace the data in iCloud with that device’s copy. You are responsible for keeping iCloud Keychain on and for your own backups, as Section 7 of the Terms of Service explains.

You may turn iCloud Sync off at any time in the App’s Options › Permissions. Syncing then stops on that device straight away, and the App asks whether to keep or delete the App’s data in iCloud. Deleting it removes it from iCloud; your other devices keep their own copies, and any of them that still has iCloud Sync on may sync its copy to iCloud again. Resetting the App while iCloud Sync is on erases the App’s data in iCloud and on each of your devices that has iCloud Sync on. You can also manage the App’s iCloud data in your Apple Account’s iCloud settings.

10. Walker Connect — what you send to other players

Walker Connect lets your walker meet another player’s walker, either with walkers nearby over your local network or with a Game Center friend. It is optional, and nothing is sent to another player unless you start a Walker Connect or accept one.

  • What is sent. When a Walker Connect takes place, the App sends the other player’s app a card about your walker: your trainer name; a player ID (your Game Center player ID when you are signed in to Game Center, or otherwise an ID made for your copy of the App); the route you are on; the walking Pokémon’s species, name, level, nature, and gender, and whether it is shiny; your step count for the day; any gift item; your team for the other player’s Trainer House; and your Trainer Card as read from your save, including your trainer name and ID, money, Pokédex count, play time, the dates your adventure started and of your Hall of Fame debut, your badges, your signature, your watts, your number of Walker Connects, your party, and your trainer’s colors.
  • How it is sent. Nearby, while the Connect screen is open, the App announces your trainer name to other devices on your local network and sends your card directly to the device you connect with. With a Game Center friend, your card is sent through Game Center. Either way, the card is encrypted with a key only the two devices share. The Developer does not receive it.
  • What the other player can do with it. The other player’s app keeps your card and shows it to them, and, if they have iCloud Sync on, keeps it in their own iCloud. Once a card has been sent, neither you nor the Developer can recall it.
  • Your choice. If you do not want to share this information, do not use Walker Connect. You can also refuse the App local network access when asked, or later in iOS Settings (or System Settings on a Mac), and choose not to use Game Center.
