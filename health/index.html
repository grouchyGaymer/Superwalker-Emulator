19. Consumer health data notice — Washington, Nevada, and similar laws

Some laws, including Washington's My Health My Data Act and Nevada's consumer health data law, require a specific notice about 'consumer health data.' This Section is that notice. It summarizes the rest of this Policy and does not change it. This Section is also published under its own separate link as the App's consumer health data privacy policy, as those laws require for that notice.

  • What is involved. step counts, and step-based achievement results, that the App reads on your device, including from Apple Health with your permission;
  • Where it comes from. your device and Apple Health, and only with the permission you give in iOS; on a device without Health, from your iPhone through iCloud Sync, if you turn it on;
  • Why it is used. to show your steps in the App and move your stroll along; only if you sign in to Game Center, to place entries on the Game Center Leaderboard and to record Game Center achievements; only if you turn on iCloud Sync, to keep your stroll and walking record the same on your devices; and only if you use Walker Connect, to show the other player your step count for the day;
  • Who receives it. the Developer does not collect it. If you sign in to Game Center, the App sends it from your device to Apple Inc. for Game Center, where Apple may display it to other players. If you turn on iCloud Sync, it is kept in your own private iCloud account, end-to-end encrypted with a key only your devices hold, so neither Apple nor the Developer can read it. If you use Walker Connect, your step count for the day is sent to the other player. It is not shared with anyone else, and it is never sold;
  • Apple protection. Apple Inc. provides Game Center and is not liable for any leaderboard entry, achievement, or step data submitted to, displayed by, or preserved in Game Center. You release Apple from any claim relating to that handling, as Section 11 explains, and you will direct any claim about information held by Apple to Apple.
  • Your consent — two separate consents, both before any sharing. (a) Collection consent: you first give iOS permission for the App to read step data, as Section 4 explains. (b) Sharing consent: separately, you give a second, distinct consent to share step data to Game Center, by choosing Use Game Center on its own page when you first set up the App (or later choosing Sign In in the App's settings) and signing in to Game Center. That page is separate from the Terms of Service, the End User License Agreement, and any general acceptance screen, and nothing on it is chosen for you. It explains, before you choose, that using Game Center earns Game Center achievements and compares your steps with friends on leaderboards. The data shared is your step counts, watts earned, and step-based achievement results; the purpose is to place entries on the Game Center Leaderboard and to record Game Center achievements, and to display them to other Game Center players as Apple permits; the recipient is Apple Inc., for Game Center; and you can withdraw consent at any time by signing out of Game Center or turning on Simulated Steps, as Section 7 explains. Steps are not sent to Game Center unless you have given both consents. iCloud Sync (Section 3) is a further, separate choice, made on its own page or in the App's settings, and is off until you turn it on. Refusing either consent does not affect your use of the App. Consent given by tapping 'I Agree' to the Terms of Service, the End User License Agreement, or this Policy alone is not consent to share health data.
  • Your rights. You may ask the Developer to confirm whether it holds consumer health data about you, to give you access to it, to delete it, and to stop sharing it, by writing to superwalkeremulator@icloud.com. The Developer will respond within forty-five (45) days, and may extend that period once by a further forty-five (45) days if the Developer tells you why. Because the Developer holds none, the Developer's answer will usually be to confirm that and to direct you to Apple. If you disagree with the answer, you may reply to ask the Developer to reconsider it, and you may contact the Attorney General of your state.
  • No sale. The Developer does not sell consumer health data, and will not sell it. A sale would require a separate signed authorization as Washington and Nevada law require, and the Developer does not seek one.
  • No geofencing. The Developer does not use a geofence around any health care facility to identify or track any person, or to collect health data or send health-related messages.
  • Separate notice. This Section is the App's consumer health data notice. It is published under its own separate link, in addition to this Policy (in the App, as the Consumer Health Data Notice in Options › Permissions), and it contains only the information those laws require for that notice.
